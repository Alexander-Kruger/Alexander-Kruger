AI & Decision Support in the Agricultural Supply Chain

Section 01

The current problem

sync_problem Information Flow Bottleneck

AI can't fix poor information flow on its own. AI and decision support systems run on data.

There firstly needs to be data that flows, this means shared systems where the parties exchange information. Then AI and DSS can turn that shared data into better decisions for each party.

Poor information can lead to farmers who plant without knowing what the processor and retailors will need in 3 months, the processor doesn’t know how much produce is coming, or when so it can’t plan staff, trucks or factory capacity. And retailors sees what customers buy, but that knowledge never reaches the farm.

waves

A good example is the bullwhip effect.

The bullwhip effect is what happens when a tiny ripple in retail sales turns into a massive tsunami by the time it hits the factory floor. A store might see a small spike in customer demand, so they get nervous and order a little extra stock just in case. Then the distributor sees that bigger order, panics, and orders even more from the wholesaler. By the time that message travels all the way up the chain, the manufacturer goes into overdrive making way too much product. Because everyone is playing a giant game of telephone instead of sharing real-time data, companies end up stuck with massive warehouse bills for unsold inventory.

Section 02

What is a DSS system?

psychology Decision Support Architecture

It is a computerised information system that helps business leaders make smarter, data-driven decisions. The DSS does not the final decision , instead it can show trends, identify problems and produce possible actions. The system could alert the manager to the possible surplus, but the manager and farmers would still decide what action to take. AI can strengthen a DSS by identifying patterns in large amounts of historical data and producing more advanced forecasts.

insights 01. Trend Sight Patterns in multi-year seasonal data
crisis_alert 02. Problem ID Surplus and deficit early-warnings
alt_route 03. Actionable Path Simulations for farmer judgment
Human-in-the-Loop Safeguard

Autonomous Guidance, Sovereign Authority

A DSS does not override agronomic craftsmanship. By elevating historical patterns without seizing final agency, farm operators retain full control over soil, timing, and stewardship.

verified_user System Boundary

Algorithmic outputs are advisory lenses designed to alleviate uncertainty, not automated edicts.

Section 03

Planting decisions

Pre-Season Pre-Harvest Calibration
Core Decision Variables

Farmers need to decide:

  • 1 what crops to plant
  • 2 how much to plan
  • 3 and when to plant

If these decisions are made without accurate demand information, farmers may produce too much or too little.

warning_amber Asymmetry Scenario: Tomatoes

For example, if farmers expect high demand for tomatoes but retailers actually need fewer tomatoes, the cooperative could end up with excess produce that cannot be sold.

Information the DSS would need

A planting DSS could use information such as:

previous retailer sales,
current and expected retailer demand,
historical crop yields,
weather forecasts,
previous harvest dates,
processor requirements,
seasonal trends,
current orders.

A predictive model could analyse historical information and estimate future demand.

Section 04

Logistics decisions

The problem

After crops are harvested, the produce needs to move from:

Farm arrow_forward Processing facility arrow_forward Retailer

The processor needs to know:

  • • where produce is located,
  • • when it will be ready,
  • • how much will be collected,
  • • which vehicles are available,
  • • and how much the processing facility can handle.

Because fresh produce can spoil, poor logistics can result in unnecessary waste and higher costs.

Documented Inefficiency

For example, of the 228 800 kg harvested, 13 500 kg (about 6%) was lost to spoilage before processing.

Information the DSS would need

A logistics DSS could use:

• expected harvest dates,
• expected harvest quantities,
• farm locations,
• truck capacity,
• vehicle availability,
• processing capacity,
• retailer delivery deadlines,
• travel distances,
• road or traffic information.
What AI/DSS could do

A DSS could combine this information and help produce a collection and delivery schedule.

Harvest Influx Matrix
Farm A 1 500 kg ready
Farm B 900 kg ready

Total available: 3 600 kg
Plant Constraint Capacity Limit

But suppose the processing facility can only process: 3 000 kg per day.

The system could warn the logistics manager that incoming produce exceeds current processing capacity. The manager could then delay one collection, organise additional processing capacity or prioritise the most perishable produce.

A DSS could also help determine an efficient route.

verified Business value

Better logistics decisions could contribute to:

  • less food spoilage,
  • lower transport costs,
  • better vehicle utilisation,
  • improved processing schedules,
  • more reliable retailer deliveries.
report_problem Limitation

The system still depends on accurate and up-to-date information. If a farmer reports that produce is ready but harvesting is delayed, the planned collection schedule may no longer work.

This is why real-time updates can be important.
Section 05

Pricing decisions

The problem

Farmers and processors need to make pricing decisions, but those decisions can be difficult when information about supply, demand and current market conditions is limited.

If farmers do not have access to appropriate market information, they may also struggle to judge whether the price being offered for their produce is reasonable.

Information the DSS could use

A pricing DSS could consider:

  • • historical selling prices,
  • • current market prices,
  • • retailer demand,
  • • available supply,
  • • production costs,
  • • transport costs,
  • • processing costs,
  • • seasonality,
  • • quality or grade of produce.
What AI/DSS could do

The system could analyse historical patterns and current information to show whether prices are increasing, decreasing or remaining relatively stable.

Scenario A: Tight Market

policy Important distinction

The AI should not simply decide:

Sell tomatoes for R24 per kilogram.

Instead, the system should provide information that helps the farmer or manager make the pricing decision.

Decision Inputs
Current market range: R21–R25/kg
Production cost: R16/kg
Demand trend: increasing

The manager can then use this information together with their own judgement.
