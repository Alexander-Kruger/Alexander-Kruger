Foundational Analysis

The Core Problem: Breaking Down Information Silos

Supply chain management (SCM) is the process of active coordination and oversight of everything to do with the sourcing, procurement, production and delivery of goods across all stages of that goods lifecycle. From raw materials to the customer buying. The main goal of SCM is to Reduce information silos across all stages of the supply chain, by integrating different systems to work in unison.

One of the goals of an Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) system is to turn information from a silo system to a more holistic approach. A silo system in an IT context, happens when the data is isolated preventing communication between different systems. When information is siloed, like in a normal chain management system each person will focus on their own operations only. Holistic system allows the integration of processes across the whole chain. Someone who uses this system understands that costs incurred at one stage of the chain are often determined by actions taken at another stage. Silos happen when, for example, the cooperative uses spreadsheets, the processor uses an older database, and the sales team uses point of sale system. We can implement a holistic approach with a centralised cloud-based ERP System.

As a manager, we need as much information as possible to make informed decisions across all sectors of the supply chain. This sector includes things like customer demand, production schedule, product routing and distribution methods as well as things like water and fertilizer supply and the overall financial plan for the season. Using ERP cloud systems, we can predict the fertilizer needs using the mobile field interface before we even start planting, saving time and resources. This sector also includes the setting of business rules for managing inventory, transportation and regulatory compliance. It is important to have good planning to ensure the supply chain matches the overall financial goals of the company.

SCM also offers an improved way of viewing the relationship between the customer and produce. The focus was always to grow as much as possible and then uncover a market to sell to. SCM both amplifies and flips this idea. SCM begins with the customer, understanding their demands for quality first and then working backward using software that uses data collected from farmers to forecast demand. Historical sales data collected from retailers could tell a farm exactly what produce sells well, which helps expand and find new markets to sell to.

The sourcing portion of the SCM refers to the procuring, obtaining of all the raw materials and services needed for production. In this case, this refers to items such as seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, machinery and labour. We need to secure reliable suppliers for this sector. This means things like contracting, negotiating contracting, managing different suppliers and handling payment. Using an ERP model and app, we can automate digital purchase orders. Since multiple farmers information will be collected as well as the demand and sales of products, it would be easy to determine which goods must be ordered as well as choosing the correct farmer to order the supplies from. Without a good supply line of both material and labour. We cannot begin production. A good sourcing solution will keep everything running smoothly.

Production Tracking & Edge Data Capture

The next step in the supply chain is production. The process of turning raw materials into ready-to-sell goods is the responsibility of the making (production) sector. This sector will include activities like planting, cultivation, harvesting and any post-harvest processing like milling, cleaning and packaging. We also need to manage production networks, equipment, facilities and quality in this step. Using the proposed Cloud ERP system, we can digitize crop yield tracking, streaming the data to the central cloud ERP database, providing critical information to help us with the next step in the chain. All in the name of making the best quality product that we can in the most efficient manner that we can. The farmer will enter the yield, crop type etc to be uploaded, and this data will be cached locally before it is uploaded to ensure no internet issue prevents uploading the data.

Logistics, IoT Sensors, and Value Optimisation

warning
2.4 Million Tonnes Annual Post-Harvest Loss in South Africa

Next, we need to get the finished product to the customer. Delivering will include the management, warehousing, transportation and distribution of our product to all our respective branches. This is an especially important sector due the perishability of the goods that we are transporting. We need to account for the spoilage of our product and to do this we are going to need an effective network of logistics, cold chain infrastructure and storage facilities. It is also worth considering the fragile nature of some of the potential products. This is why we use our ERP system to track our logistics in real time as well as tracking cold temperature containers to provide us critical information that can be used when logistics fail. It has the potential to prevent failures as we can monitor the temperature to maintain optimal temperature. It is important to deliver the product quality that the customers demand and to prevent spoilage as much as possible. By keeping track of spoilage rates, we can also identify how long on average crops spoil and if there are any outliers that might suggest a bigger problem.

One of the key advantages of the SCM model is value optimizations. While normally, a company would aim to reduce costs within a single point on the chain at a time. SCM allows us to focus on the chain as a whole. This allows us to consider trade-offs throughout the whole process. For example, spending more on sensors for the cold storage systems might cost more money now however, it would feed data to back into our Cloud ERP, that would allow us to detect faults and fix equipment immediately without first incurring a loss. What does effective SCM look like in the modern world? Every year more businesses are adopting the SCM model to improve on all aspects of the agriculture chain. One of the biggest problems in agriculture, post-harvest loss is costing the industry millions every year. In South Africa we lose an estimated 2.4 million tonnes of food every year due to post-harvest loss. SCM serves to help improve coordination at every step, thereby reducing lost food. For example, sensors on cold storage systems would prevent spoilage without first incurring losses. It has the potential to save us millions of tonnes of food annually.

Using the SCM system has benefits for everyone involved by using the optimisation approach of managing to save costs. We can improve farmer income as well as reducing market cost. Thereby saving money for everyone involved.

Lastly, we focus on the returning of goods, Products that might be defective or inventory that might be in excess. It is also worth returning reusable packaging like containers and pallets. This matters as there needs to be a way to handle rejected stock that doesn’t meet quality standards. This sector also includes customer support. Our ERP model will handle digital receipts for the flow of goods back to us. This information will be fed to the database so we can see how much product was sent back on the system. A digital refund will be given to clients who returned goods and it will be flagged for inspection.

End-to-End Digital Traceability

The SCM model also helps companies in ensuring food quality to the customer. It allows for better quality control throughout the whole chain. We can track the product from raw materials to the customers hands. This can be implemented with the use of barcode or QR code generation created by the ERP system at the processing facility, allowing a retailer to scan a specific batch and trace it back to a specific farmer. A system like this builds trust between the consumer and provider as well as the national food standards.

Technical Deployment & IS Integration Challenges

Buying a cloud-based ERP does come with a lot of challenges, firstly people do not want change and will resist it. To ease this burden sufficient planning with all parties involved will be necessary before implementation. Secondly, if old or legacy systems do not have a application programming interface, for example old database software, we cannot immediately send the data to the central cloud database. The database might need to be reconstructed to assure future compatibility. Thirdly, we will need to analyse the as-is spreadsheets to make sure the data in the old and new fields are the same format, and if they aren’t we can use artificial intelligence to convert the data fields in the desired format. Lastly, the internet in South Africa is not always the best; to solve this the application interface will need to include offline caching to ensure that the information will eventually be sent.
